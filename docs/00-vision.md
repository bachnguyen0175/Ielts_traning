# Vision

> Status: 🟡 draft

## The problem

Candidates who "know the material" still underperform on IELTS. The gap is
rarely knowledge — it's **test-day conditions**: single-play audio, unforgiving
timing, an unfamiliar computer-delivered interface, and the accumulated fatigue
of a ~2h45m sitting. Most prep tools drill *content* (question banks, vocab,
grammar). Few rehearse *conditions*.

## Our thesis

> If we reproduce test-day conditions with high fidelity, candidates arrive
> desensitized to the pressure and perform closer to their true ability.

This is a differentiator, not a feature. The interface, the timing rules, and
the "no feedback until the end" discipline **are** the product.

## Target users

- **Primary:** Academic IELTS candidates (university / professional
  registration) who have studied the content and now need realistic rehearsal.
- **Secondary (later):** Tutors/schools wanting to assign authentic mocks and
  review submissions.

## How we win (differentiators)

1. **Condition fidelity** — play-once listening, section-level timers that match
   the real test, the computer-delivered UI (highlight, review screen,
   navigation).
2. **Full-sitting endurance** — the complete 2h45m mock as the hero experience,
   not just isolated drills.
3. **Credible scoring (phased)** — objective auto-scoring first; band-criteria
   scoring for Writing/Speaking added once validated against real outcomes.

## Non-goals (for now)

- Not a content-teaching platform (no lessons/courses in MVP).
- Not General Training (Academic first).
- Not a live AI examiner in the MVP (see [scope](./01-scope-mvp.md)).

## Success metrics (draft — 🔴 needs targets)

- **Activation:** % of signups who complete a full mock.
- **Fidelity proof:** self-reported "felt like the real test" ≥ target.
- **Retention:** repeat mocks per user over 4 weeks.
- **Scoring credibility (phase 2+):** correlation between our band scores and
  users' actual reported IELTS results.

## Open questions

- Do we need a question-authoring pipeline, or licensed/seeded content first?
